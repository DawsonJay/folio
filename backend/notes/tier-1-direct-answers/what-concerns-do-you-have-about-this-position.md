# What concerns do you have about this position?

The biggest ones are self-direction and meaningful work. I need to be given a problem and the freedom to solve it the way I think best. Micromanagement — and the distrust it signals — produces worse outcomes. I also need to feel the work matters, even if that's as specific as pulling my weight effectively for my team rather than building something the company will never use.

I hesitate at low-trust signals: heavy monitoring of how people work matters less in itself than what it usually signals about the culture behind it.

To find out if a role is right for me I ask about the team — and listen for whether the interviewer sounds excited and proud when they answer. A recruiter once couldn't stop talking about how good the team was; he really wanted me to meet the lead developer because he thought we'd approach problems similarly and get on well. That kind of enthusiasm about people rather than product or perks tells me a lot. The counter-signal is a team where people feel used by management or take the maximum time off because work feels unbearable — as a symptom of collective cynicism rather than genuine need.
