# How do you approach UI/UX design?

The Integrations Dashboard I rebuilt at Nurtur has run 3+ years with zero maintenance; the original was overcrowded, broke often, and wasn't pleasant to use. I conveyed the same information in a much more intuitive way that didn't get in the way of user flow. On the Nexus Dashboard I represented graphs and major components as blocks in a grid - a library of components that could be swapped in and out, which made the design flexible for the major changes we knew were coming.

I start by talking to people. I want to find out the real intent behind the task, what the data looks like, what they want to use it for. How it will be used should inform the design, not the other way around.

Then I make sketches and notes on paper. When I'm confident, I make a loose Figma to show the team who'll use it and get their feedback; usually they have useful input and I go back to paper to improve. Each stage takes more effort and is harder to change, so I get a solid design early rather than diving into code and figuring the design out later.

A lot of UX is about feeling. If a design makes you feel even a slight twist of confusion or annoyance it has to be changed. Users might not consciously notice, but it still influences their experience. My art background gives me that sensitivity - in art you create a feeling or message, and you build awareness of what it's communicating on an unconscious level.
