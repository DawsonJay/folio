# What challenges did you face transitioning to tech?

Leaving fine art was incredibly difficult. I knew society would see it as failure or laziness. My family wouldn't understand. The uncertainty of not knowing what I would do next was overwhelming, and having to move back in with my parents while figuring out my next steps was challenging.

But I knew fine art was wrong as a career for me. I loved the creativity but struggled with how subjective art was, especially its value. Success depended on public opinion, not skill or the value of the work itself. I also needed more intellectual challenge. Art was creative but didn't exercise my logical side.

In the time after leaving art, I got interested in coding and realized it fulfilled what I loved about art plus my need for intellectual challenges. As a more practical pursuit, coding success wasn't determined by public vibes but by skill and the value a piece of code produced.

The transition happened through self-teaching first (books, Codecademy, Udemy courses - making half-formed games), then eventually Ironhack bootcamp in May 2020. I tried Heriot Watt University for AI but left after one year - it was too slow and disconnected from reality. I researched the job market and found portfolios mattered more than degrees. I could build my own portfolio faster than a four-year course.
