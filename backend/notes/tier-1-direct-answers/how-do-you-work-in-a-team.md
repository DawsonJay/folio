# How do you work in a team?

I prefer working in teams. I spent 15 months as a freelance web developer, and while I enjoyed being responsible for my own projects, I discovered that I need teammates to share solutions with, people to discuss technical challenges with, and the ability to support others' growth. Working alone took that away.

Having a team gives a spread of experience, allows me to focus on one aspect at a time, or manage the project as a whole not both, and provides a buffer between me and the general public. As I've mentioned, it's also important to me to be able to support the people around me, and working alone took that away.

I prefer working in small teams (3-4 people) where I can have focused collaboration but also work independently when needed. I can do deep work on my own tasks, then come together with the team to coordinate, share solutions, and get feedback. The key is having the option to work independently when that's most effective, but always having teammates available for collaboration, support, and shared problem-solving.

My leadership style is what people have called the "team dad" approach. It's not about authority or position - it's about bringing people together, creating spaces where people feel safe to learn and grow, and genuinely caring about the people I work with. I've successfully mentored 3 backend developers transitioning to full-stack roles at Nurtur. I make people feel rock-solid confidence in their ability to learn something, even if it seems difficult at first. Fear and insecurity are the biggest barriers to learning, not capability.

I naturally end up in a connecting role on teams. I bridge gaps between people - helping backend developers understand what frontend developers need, translating technical constraints into language non-technical stakeholders can understand, and generally making sure people are communicating effectively. I've found that most conflicts come from misunderstanding rather than genuine disagreement, and often just facilitating clear communication resolves things.

My leadership philosophy is that teams work best when everyone feels they have a stake in the outcome and the psychological safety to contribute honestly. I try to create that environment by being genuinely interested in others' perspectives, admitting when I don't know something, and treating mistakes as learning opportunities rather than failures. When I have technical authority in an area, I'll use it to guide decisions, but I always explain my reasoning and I'm open to being convinced otherwise if someone has a good argument.

I prefer small teams with consensus decision-making and shared project ownership. I don't want to be the sole decision-maker - I want everyone to have input and feel ownership of the work. This creates better outcomes because multiple perspectives catch problems and generate better solutions.

What makes my leadership style effective is that it's authentic. I genuinely care about helping people grow and succeed. I'm not performing leadership - I'm naturally drawn to supporting others and creating environments where everyone can do their best work. This authenticity makes people trust me and want to work with me.
