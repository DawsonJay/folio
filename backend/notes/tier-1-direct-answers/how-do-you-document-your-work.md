# How do you document your work?

Documentation solves one core problem: the moment you come back to code you haven't touched in weeks. On Nexus I built a two-layer system — a running dev log updated at milestones (decisions made, problems hit, how they were solved), and an Obsidian-style vault of atomic notes distilled from those logs into a navigable reference. Both were written for multiple audiences simultaneously: a future developer joining cold, a future me returning after time away, and an AI assistant querying for context rather than re-reading raw code.

The practical test is whether it saves time. There was a legacy page at BriefYourMarket I was regularly called back to fix — old code, quirky, easy to forget between incidents. My dev logs let me scan them and recover the tricks without rediscovering everything from scratch each time. That saved hours at unpredictable intervals.

Documentation also protects against knowledge loss. Too often one developer holds all the context on why a system was built a certain way — then notice is handed in and there's a scramble. Writing with that specific failure in mind changes what you choose to record. The goal isn't comprehensive coverage; it's the things that cost time when they're missing.
