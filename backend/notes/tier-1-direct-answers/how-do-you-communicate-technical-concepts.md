# How do you communicate technical concepts?

I explain complex concepts by relating them to familiar experiences. My background in art, teaching kayaking, and bridging teams at Nurtur developed this skill. When teaching CSS to backend developers, I didn't explain syntax - I related it to patterns they knew. CSS selectors like database queries, layout like data structures, cascading like inheritance. That connection helped it click.

The biggest barrier to learning isn't capability, it's fear. I create psychological safety by showing that confusion is normal and temporary. We all felt this way starting out. Once people feel safe not knowing, actual learning happens.

On the Integrations Dashboard, I bridged backend developers and sales teams who thought in completely different terms. Sales worked with spreadsheets and customer needs. Backend worked with Azure functions and C# code. I could translate between them - explaining backend constraints to sales in terms of workflow impact, translating sales requests to backend in terms of data structures and API requirements.

I use metaphors and analogies constantly. Code organization like physical objects, visual metaphors from art for abstract patterns, technical systems compared to natural systems. These connections help people understand new concepts by linking to existing knowledge.

When communicating with non-technical stakeholders, I focus on what they need to accomplish, not how code works. Show interfaces, get usability feedback, explain constraints in business terms. Listen first - often what people think they need isn't what they actually need.
