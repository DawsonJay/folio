# What kind of problems do you find most interesting to solve?

Working inside real constraints. Atlantis — a project to photograph underwater ruins with a Raspberry Pi — is the clearest example. The limitations of water pressure, battery life, and cost pushed me into solutions I'd never have found with an unlimited budget. Constraints force cleverness. Projects with vague specifications and more money than ideas tend to produce bloated code where the answer to every problem is just more resources.

The other thing that consistently interests me is thinking about code as an architecture — the whole system as an ecosystem. On Nexus I structured things in modular layers so new pages could be assembled from existing building blocks. Shared components meant the foundational code was bulletproof, and each new screen dramatically reduced where bugs could appear. Thinking about how a system will respond and grow over time is genuinely fascinating — it's almost like urban planning, making sure the roads go in the right places before the city grows around them.

What drains me is work where quality doesn't matter and won't outlast the sprint. I find it hard to care about code I know will cause problems in a month. I build things I can stand behind.
