# Tell me about your educational background

My educational background is pretty non-traditional. I completed a Foundation Year in Illustration at the University of Derby in 2014, but I didn't continue to the full degree. I realized that while I loved the creative process, I needed something that combined creativity with intellectual challenges. That's what drew me to coding.

I tried studying AI at Heriot-Watt University from 2017 to 2019, but found it too slow and disconnected from reality. I had already been coding for a few years by that point, and the coursework felt like it was holding me back rather than accelerating my growth. I completed 2 years of coursework and earned a Certificate of Merit for my first year - strong academic performance. I gained foundational knowledge in machine learning, neural networks, algorithms, and databases that would later inform my work on AI/ML projects like WhatNow, moh-ami, and Folio.

I left Heriot-Watt to pursue more practical, project-based learning that aligned with my goal-driven learning style. I researched the job market and found that while a lot of places liked you to have a qualification, what they really required was a portfolio of projects proving that you could do the work. I knew that I could create my own portfolio far quicker than the four-year course would, and I could save a lot of money on a degree that wasn't even needed.

I did an Ironhack bootcamp in May 2020, which was a strategic decision to bridge my personal project work into structured professional skills. The bootcamp was full-stack focused, covering React, Node.js, MongoDB, and professional development practices. It was delivered remotely during COVID-19 lockdowns, which meant learning to collaborate effectively in a distributed environment. Coming out of it, I had a portfolio of projects and the confidence to apply for junior developer positions. Within a few months, I secured my first professional role as a Junior Web Developer at BriefYourMarket.

The bootcamp validated my self-taught foundation. While other students were learning programming concepts for the first time, I was building on years of prior experience. I focused on professional practices - working in teams, code reviews, deployment, real project constraints - rather than basic syntax.

I've also completed an IELTS exam with an overall band score of 8.5 (C2 level), with perfect scores in listening, reading, and speaking.

My educational journey taught me that practical, hands-on learning works better for me than theoretical academic study. I learn best by doing, not by studying. This is why bootcamp was more valuable than university for me, and why my portfolio projects continue to teach me more than courses ever did.
