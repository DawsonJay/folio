# Tell me about your leadership style

People have called me the "team dad" and it's a description that feels accurate. My leadership style is about bringing people together, creating spaces where people feel safe to learn and grow, and genuinely caring about the people I work with.

At Nurtur, I've mentored three backend developers who wanted to expand their skills into frontend development. One of them had never worked with CSS before and was intimidated by it. We did pair programming sessions where I'd explain CSS concepts by relating them to backend patterns they already understood - "It's like selecting data from a database, but you're selecting elements from the DOM." That connection helped it click for them. Now all three of them work confidently across the full stack, which has made our team more flexible and effective.

Teaching is about understanding where someone's starting from, what they're trying to achieve, and building a bridge between those two points. I try to make people feel rock-solid confidence in their ability to learn something, even if it seems difficult at first. Fear and insecurity are the biggest barriers to learning, not capability.

My approach to mentoring is patient and encouraging. I'll work through fundamentals, build understanding step by step, and celebrate improvement rather than fixating on perfection. When someone asks a question, I try to answer in a way that helps them understand the underlying principles so they can solve similar problems themselves in the future.

I naturally end up in a connecting role on teams. I bridge gaps between people - helping backend developers understand what frontend developers need, translating technical constraints into language non-technical stakeholders can understand, and making sure people are communicating effectively. Most conflicts come from misunderstanding rather than genuine disagreement, and often just facilitating clear communication resolves things.
