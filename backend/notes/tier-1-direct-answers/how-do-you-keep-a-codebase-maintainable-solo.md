# How do you keep a codebase maintainable when you're the only one working in it?

Working heavily with AI sharpened my solo discipline more than anything else. AI makes sweeping changes without full context, so to stay on top of it I developed tight habits: small modular components where changes only affect one contained area, tests around core mechanics to catch drift, careful review of everything changed. Those habits are general now — AI was the pressure that made them non-negotiable.

The core principles are readable code over clever code, clear naming, and structure that orients a new reader quickly. A teacher at Ironhack loved cramming solutions onto one line. Clever, but often near-unreadable. Good code should feel like there was no other sensible way to write it.

For future me specifically, I keep dev logs. Coming back to an area weeks later I scan the log rather than re-reading the code. On the Nexus Dashboard that also captured the architectural reasoning — the why behind decisions — which is the first thing that gets forgotten. The test of good solo habits is whether returning to your own code months later feels like reading someone else's or feels like picking up exactly where you left off.
