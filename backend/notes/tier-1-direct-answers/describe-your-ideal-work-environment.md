# Describe your ideal work environment

The working situation I've found most effective is a small setup where I have real autonomy over my area, a clear point of accountability, and at least one other person to collaborate closely with. The Nexus Dashboard at Nurtur was the closest I've had to that professionally — just me and one backend developer, the frontend design entirely mine, structured so I could present progress to the head developer for approval. That combination of freedom and accountability worked well for me.

What makes work feel genuinely good is being close enough to the impact to see it. On both Nexus and the Integrations Dashboard I was in direct communication with the sales team throughout — they were excited about what we were building, and with the Integrations Dashboard I got to watch their working lives get easier as it released in stages. That kind of feedback loop is what inspires me to make something excellent rather than just functional. I work better when I can see who I'm building for.

The other thing I need is trust. Not in an abstract sense — specifically, the freedom to design and build something the way I think it should be done, with someone whose judgment I respect checking it at the right moments. That's different from being left alone, and different from being supervised closely. It's the middle ground where the best work happens.

I recognised this dynamic in university group projects too — small groups, clear goals, individual ownership of a piece. The professional version just has higher stakes and better feedback.

I've also learned that the right environment doesn't always arrive fully formed. Being someone who raises problems directly, mentors teammates, and cares about how the team functions is part of how you build it. I'm not waiting for the ideal conditions — I'm part of creating them.
