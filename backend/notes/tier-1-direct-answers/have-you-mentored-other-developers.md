# Have you mentored other developers?

Yes. At Nurtur I mentored three backend developers who were transitioning into full-stack roles as part of a programme to broaden the team's capabilities. One had never worked with CSS and found it genuinely intimidating. I approached it by connecting CSS concepts to things they already understood from the backend — selectors work like database queries, layout systems have a similar logic to data structures. That kind of bridge-building is what actually helps something click rather than just adding to the pile of things someone feels they should already know.

All three became confident across the full stack. When someone new joined a team and was struggling, I was usually the one people pointed them toward. I think that's partly because I'm patient, and partly because I try to answer questions in a way that builds understanding rather than just solving the immediate problem — so the next similar problem they can handle themselves.

Beyond formal mentoring, I tend to bridge gaps between people naturally. At Nurtur that often meant helping backend developers understand why the frontend needed something a certain way, or translating technical constraints for the sales team in terms they could actually work with.
