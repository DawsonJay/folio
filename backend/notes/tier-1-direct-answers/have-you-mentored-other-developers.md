# Have you mentored other developers?

Yes, I've successfully mentored 3 backend developers at Nurtur transitioning to full-stack roles. One had never worked with CSS and was intimidated. We did pair programming where I explained CSS by relating it to backend patterns they knew - CSS selectors like database queries, layout like data structures. That connection helped it click. Now all three work confidently across the full stack, making our team more flexible and effective.

Teaching isn't dumping information. It's understanding where someone starts, what they're trying to achieve, and building a bridge between those points. Fear and insecurity are the biggest learning barriers, not capability. I create confidence that they can learn this, even if it seems difficult.

My approach is patient and encouraging. Work through fundamentals, build understanding step by step, celebrate improvement over perfection. When someone asks a question, I answer in ways that help them understand underlying principles, not just solve the immediate problem. They can then solve similar problems independently.

Beyond formal mentoring, I naturally bridge gaps between people. I help backend developers understand frontend needs, translate technical constraints for non-technical stakeholders, and facilitate clear communication. Most conflicts come from misunderstanding rather than genuine disagreement.

Watching people grow is what I find rewarding about leadership. Those three developers gained confidence building features they couldn't tackle before. I'd rather create ten bright sparks in others than shine alone. Supportive leadership focused on bringing out capability.
