# Have you worked with strict processes?

Yes, and I've worked across the full range. On the Nexus Dashboard I was in a small 2-person team building an internal tool, so the process was light — we moved fast and I was responsible for checking my own work. On the Build product, which the company's business depended on, each work item went through formal discussion, peer code review, deployment to a dev branch for testing, QA, and then a closely monitored release to main. Multiple gates before anything reached production.

Both made sense in context and neither bothered me. The light process meant faster iterations and more freedom, but it also meant more personal responsibility for quality. The heavy process caught real problems often enough to make the overhead obviously worthwhile — and releasing into a high-stakes environment with that many layers of protection is actually less stressful, because the risk is shared across the team. You do your best work, trust your peers to catch what you missed, and return the favour on their reviews.

My usual analogy: airlocks. Slow and frustrating on Earth, but you'd be very glad of them on Mars. The right process depends entirely on the environment.
