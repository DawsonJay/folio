# Tell me about a successful team project

The Integrations Dashboard was a successful team project where I worked alongside 3 backend developers to build an internal tool that's been in production for 3+ years with zero maintenance. I was the only frontend developer, building the React/TypeScript frontend while they worked on backend services and APIs. Sometimes a backend developer would implement a dashboard backend feature when time was tight - this flexibility meant we could respond to priorities without role boundary blockers.

I was basically responsible for creating the dashboard. The frontend was entirely me, and I did a fair portion of backend work as well. This full-stack ownership meant I could make architectural decisions and ensure frontend and backend worked together seamlessly.

The dashboard succeeded on three criteria. It reached completion - we scoped appropriately, built systematically, and shipped to the sales team who needed it. It gave the intended value - quick customer lookups, integration status visibility, and troubleshooting tools without requiring database knowledge. It kept working - 3+ years in production with no maintenance and zero failures. It was well-made and stable.

What made success possible was clear scope, cross-functional collaboration, ownership and autonomy, continuous feedback loops, and simple solid architecture. We knew what problem we were solving and for whom. Having backend developers who could step in when needed prevented blockers. Clear ownership meant everyone knew their responsibilities. I talked at length with both sales team users and backend team throughout development. The crystal formation architecture - simple clean components combining into sophisticated structure - made the system easy to understand, easy to modify, and resistant to bugs.

Three years of zero-maintenance production use is the real measure of success.
