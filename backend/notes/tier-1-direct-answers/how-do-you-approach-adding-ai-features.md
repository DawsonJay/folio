# How do you approach adding AI features?

Start with the problem, not the technology. The most common mistake I've seen is adding AI before there's a clearly defined problem for it to solve. AI tends to expand to fill whatever space you give it — it can seem like it could do almost anything, which makes it seductive to reach for when something more specific and cheaper would actually work better.

Where AI is the right answer, I try to contain it to the smallest scope that captures the genuine value. That means keeping the non-deterministic parts isolated in code that's designed around the constraint of not being testable the way regular logic is. The frontend developer's job with AI behind the interface is essentially to create a reliable container around something inherently unreliable.

My rule of thumb from building WhatNow and Folio: if you can describe the exact problem the AI is solving in a single sentence, you're probably on the right track. If the answer involves a lot of "and also..." the scope has drifted. The wasabi analogy holds: used precisely in the right place it elevates everything around it, used broadly it just makes everything strong-tasting and unpredictable.
