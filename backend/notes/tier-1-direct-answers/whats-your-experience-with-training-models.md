# What's your experience with training models?

My experience is focused on using pre-trained models and fine-tuning algorithms rather than training deep learning models from scratch. For WhatNow, I implemented custom contextual bandit algorithms that learn from user feedback - the Session AI and Base AI system continuously trains on user choices to improve recommendations. This is practical ML engineering: designing learning systems, tuning hyperparameters, balancing exploration vs exploitation, and maintaining model performance in production.

For embeddings, I use pre-trained sentence transformers (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) rather than training embedding models from scratch. I understand how embeddings work, how to evaluate quality for specific use cases, and how to integrate them into production systems. This pragmatic approach ships working AI-powered applications quickly rather than reinventing AI infrastructure.

I explored model training on the Cirrus weather prediction project - training models on historical weather data, validating predictions, tuning hyperparameters. The project ultimately failed due to data quality issues, but the experience taught me practical ML constraints: data quality matters more than algorithm sophistication, real-world prediction is harder than tutorial datasets, and computational resources for training at scale require careful optimization.

My approach favors using existing AI services (OpenAI API, embedding services, established frameworks) and focusing effort on product design, user experience, and integration. I'm comfortable training custom algorithms when needed (contextual bandits, reinforcement learning), but I'm not a research scientist training novel deep learning architectures. I'm an engineer integrating AI capabilities into production web applications.
