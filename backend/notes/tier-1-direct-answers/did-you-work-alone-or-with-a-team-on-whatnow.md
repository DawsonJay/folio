# Did you work alone or with a team on WhatNow?

I built WhatNow entirely independently - from concept through architecture, development, deployment, and ongoing maintenance. It's a solo project demonstrating full ownership across the entire stack.

Working solo meant making all architectural decisions, solving all technical challenges, and handling all deployment and maintenance. There's no one else to catch mistakes or share debugging sessions, so the code quality and system robustness needed to be solid from the start. The system has run for months with minimal issues because I built defensively.

Solo work on WhatNow contrasts with team projects at Nurtur. At Nurtur, I worked with backend developers, designers, and product teams on projects like the Integrations Dashboard and Email Editor. Team projects involve coordination, communication, and dividing responsibilities. Solo projects require full-stack ownership and self-sufficiency.

Both approaches offer value. Solo projects demonstrate complete technical ownership, self-direction, and ability to see projects through from concept to deployment without external support. Team projects demonstrate collaboration, communication, and working within established processes and constraints. WhatNow proves I can build production systems independently when needed.

The solo nature of WhatNow is actually an advantage for demonstrating skills. Everything in the system - architecture decisions, code quality, UX design, deployment strategy - reflects my work directly. There's no ambiguity about what I contributed versus what teammates built.
