# What do you know about computer vision or object detection?

Genuine computer vision experience is limited: I built Jam Hot, a fruit classification project that hit 86% validation accuracy then dropped to 0% in real-world use. That failure was instructive — validation and real-world performance can diverge sharply, and an interface that doesn't surface this honestly does more damage than one that admits uncertainty.

Conceptually I understand what computer vision is doing: identifying subjects of relevance in images — objects, faces, or more abstract scene understanding like how multiple elements are interacting. Historically one of the hardest tasks for software; the processing power once achieved is what makes it so valuable at scale — huge amounts of footage processed quickly in ways no human team could manage.

If I joined a CV team today my immediate contribution would be frontend. In parallel I'd pick up bugs and small tasks touching the CV layer — the same immersion approach I used when I joined the Integrations backend team at Nurtur knowing almost nothing about their stack, and left it comfortable enough to pick up solo backend jobs. Company-specific questions I'd ask colleagues; anything publicly researchable I'd pursue in my own time.
