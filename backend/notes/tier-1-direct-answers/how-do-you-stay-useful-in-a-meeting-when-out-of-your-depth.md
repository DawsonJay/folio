# How do you stay useful in a meeting when you're out of your depth on the topic?

My instinct is to ask questions — higher-level ones first. Intent behind the feature, why it's a good idea, what value it gives. I start with questions I can understand the answers to without pulling the meeting into a long detour of explaining everything from scratch. Those overview questions often surface useful points — sometimes people miss obvious things like whether a feature is actually useful to users rather than just technically interesting.

A clear example was joining the Integrations team as the resident frontend developer in a group of experienced backend engineers working in systems they'd known for years. I was regularly out of my depth. The dashboard work meant I was still adding real value — so I wasn't making every meeting about my education. Standups were useful for building context gradually. In planning meetings I kept questions broad: what is this for, why does it matter. Over time that kind of patient immersion is what moved me from genuinely out of my depth to being able to pick up small backend jobs when the dashboard work slowed.
