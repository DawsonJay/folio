# What do you do when a design doesn't work in code?

In professional work I've never had anything major — usually it's something small, a button behaviour or a layout edge case. When that happens I have a quick conversation with the designer. I come prepared with why it can't be done as specified and a couple of alternatives that achieve the same intent. The framing matters: the designer and I want the same thing, which is to express their original idea. So it's a problem to solve together, not a negotiation over who's right.

For the internal tools I designed myself, I avoided most large problems before they started. Knowing how hard something is to build from experience means I don't design things that are going to be painful to code. I'd also talk to users before committing to a direction — usually in separate calls rather than a group, so I could see where their instincts aligned and where they diverged. That surfaced problems early.

The two root causes of design problems in my experience: not knowing what's actually buildable, and not really knowing what the end user needs. The second one is worse — you can end up polishing the wrong thing entirely.
