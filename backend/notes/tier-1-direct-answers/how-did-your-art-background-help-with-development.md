# How did your art background help with development?

My art background gives me intuition about code structure and UX that purely technical training doesn't provide. I can sense when code structure feels right or wrong, when something is "done" versus needing more work, and when UX feels natural versus forced.

This intuition acts as a compass in debugging and problem-solving. On the WhatNow project, I could feel when the recommendation algorithm wasn't quite right before metrics confirmed it. My gut often points me to the right area of code. I follow intuition with rigorous logical investigation - the feeling is a compass, not proof, but it accelerates finding problems.

For UI/UX, art background means I think about visual balance, color theory, and how people interact with visual information. The theatrical diorama system for my portfolio website - layered SVG animations and color gradients from dark tech surfaces to warm paper lantern tones - came from artistic foundation. I think about how systems look and feel to users, not just whether they function correctly.

Art taught me when something is done. In painting or pottery, there's a gut feeling when things are exactly right, when anything else you do makes things worse. That skill took years to develop through art. Now I can sense when code is stable, functional, well-written - when more polish is perfectionism versus being practical.

The creative approach transfers to component architecture and system design. There's an aesthetic to good code - clean composition, elegant solutions, systems that feel naturally structured. My "crystal formation" architecture philosophy is a visual metaphor from art background.
