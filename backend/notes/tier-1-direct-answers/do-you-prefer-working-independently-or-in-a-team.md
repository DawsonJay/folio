# Do you prefer working independently or in a team?

I prefer working in teams. I spent 15 months as a freelance web developer, and while I enjoyed being responsible for my own projects, I discovered that I need teammates to share solutions with, people to discuss technical challenges with, and the ability to support others' growth. Working alone took that away.

Having a team gives a spread of experience, allows me to focus on one aspect at a time, or manage the project as a whole not both, and provides a buffer between me and the general public. As I've mentioned, it's also important to me to be able to support the people around me, and working alone took that away.

I prefer working in small teams (3-4 people) where I can have focused collaboration but also work independently when needed. I can do deep work on my own tasks, then come together with the team to coordinate, share solutions, and get feedback. The key is having the option to work independently when that's most effective, but always having teammates available for collaboration, support, and shared problem-solving.

I don't want constant collaboration - that's exhausting. But I also don't want complete isolation - that's draining in a different way. The ideal is a small team where I can do focused independent work but always have teammates available for collaboration, problem-solving, and mutual support. That's the balance that works for me.

Freelancing taught me that I need teammates to share solutions with. I'm more motivated when I can show someone a clever approach and they understand why it's elegant. The joy of solving a problem is amplified when I can share it with someone who appreciates the solution. I need people to share difficult challenges with - problems are easier when you can discuss them with teammates. I need to be able to help people grow - that's part of what makes work meaningful. I want to focus on what I'm good at, not handle every aspect of every project. I want to do technical work, not manage client expectations and scope changes.

These needs are met in team environments, not in solo freelancing. That's why I prefer teams - not because I can't work independently, but because teams provide the collaboration, support, and focus that make work sustainable and meaningful.

I can work independently when needed. I'm comfortable with self-direction and I can handle solo projects. But I prefer teams because they provide collaboration, support, focus, and the ability to help others grow. These things make work better for me, and I think they make my work better too.
