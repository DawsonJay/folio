# What does good design mean to you?

Good design answers the questions you have — including the ones you didn't know you had — and lets you take actions with barely a thought. It feels invisible, like there was no other way it could have been made.

The clearest illustration I have is the Integrations Dashboard at Nurtur. The first version was an engineer-first pass: it got the data visible and truthful, which was the right priority early on. But it didn't shape pages around how a salesperson actually scans information, prioritises tasks, or completes a workflow. Every page felt overwhelming. It wasn't answering the unspoken questions users arrived with. Good data, poor design.

A less professional example — but one that changed how I think — was helping a technophobic friend set up Discord. Something I found trivial took hours and genuinely stressed her out. Every obstacle made her feel stupid. Up until that point I'd been unconsciously designing for developers. After that I started designing for technophobes: if something feels frictionless to someone who finds technology upsetting, it's excellent for everyone else too.

Both experiences point at the same thing: good design isn't about providing all the information. It's about surfacing the right information, in the right order, so the user barely has to think. You know you're there when users can't tell you're doing it.
