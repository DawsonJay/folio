# How do you prioritize tasks?

I prioritize based on value versus effort and user impact. High value, low effort comes first - quick wins that provide real benefit. High value, high effort gets investment when justified. The Integrations Dashboard was high effort but the value - 3+ years in production serving the sales team daily with zero maintenance - made it worth the investment.

I align everything with goals. If a task doesn't move me toward meaningful objectives, I question whether it's worth doing. I think in terms of real outcomes, not activities. Not "write more code" but "make the sales team 50% more productive." Not "add a feature" but "help users discover experiences they'll love." This keeps work focused on what actually matters.

Dependencies and blockers affect priority. Sometimes low-value tasks become high priority because they unblock high-value work. The foundation blocks architecture for Nexus was prerequisite work that enabled faster dashboard development later. If my teammates can't proceed without something from me, that moves up even if it's not the most interesting work.

I protect deep focus time for complex problems that need sustained attention. I group similar tasks to minimize context switching - if I need to fix three CSS issues, I do them together rather than scattering them across days. When everything feels urgent, I challenge the urgency. Is this actually urgent or just loud? Real urgency means significant negative consequences if delayed.

Long-term, I prioritize projects that align with my values and advance my career trajectory. I'm building from web development foundations toward AI/ML integration for web applications. WhatNow taught contextual bandits. moh-ami taught LLM integration. Each project strategically builds skills for where I'm going.
