# What would you want to find out in your first week?

My first priority is to start providing value as quickly as possible, so I learn what I need for that and expand outward from there. In practice that means taking whatever initial task I'm given, learning as much as possible about that specific area of the codebase, and building a small network of people I can ask questions of as my understanding grows. Trying to understand everything before contributing would be paralysing — the learning compounds faster when it's attached to real work.

What I focus on first is the task, not the full system. The release process matters, for example, but it matters when I reach that stage — not before. When I get there I ask: is there peer review, QA, a standard route to production? Then I follow it. I don't need the full map before I take the first step.

For building out who to ask: I find a few people across the team rather than directing everything at one person, and I match questions to whoever has the relevant expertise. Seniority matters — if anyone on the team can answer something, there's no reason to take up the most senior developer's time with it.
