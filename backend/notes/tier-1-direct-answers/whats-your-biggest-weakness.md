# What's your biggest weakness?

My biggest weakness is probably my tendency to get completely absorbed in a project. When I'm deep in something, I can focus on it to the exclusion of everything else. I'll stay up until early morning trying to get one thing perfect. That intensity is actually what drives my best work - it's what created the Integrations Dashboard, an internal tool that's been running for over 3 years without any maintenance. But like any strength, it needs balance to be sustainable.

The Ironhack bootcamp taught me this the hard way. I decided to focus on it entirely because I needed it to start my career. I put everything I had into it, prioritizing the bootcamp above other activities and commitments. It worked - I completed it successfully and it launched my career. But I learned that this level of intensity, while effective short-term, isn't sustainable long-term. I learned that I needed to maintain other parts of my life even during intensive periods.

I've learned to manage this. I block out time for exercise and seeing friends as scheduled commitments, not optional activities. I create a stronger separation between work and personal life. These aren't things I'll do if I have time - they're protected commitments that I treat with the same importance as critical work. Sustainable excellence requires maintaining health, relationships, and perspective alongside technical work.

I don't see my intensity as something to eliminate. It's part of who I am and it drives my best work. Instead, I see it as something to manage and channel productively. The intensity that can be unsustainable when unchecked is the same intensity that creates excellent work when balanced.

I also want to improve my Python and AI/ML skills. I've used Python for AI/ML projects like WhatNow, an activity recommendation system, and moh-ami, a French learning tool, but I want to deepen that expertise. I'm particularly interested in integrating AI capabilities into web applications - things like intelligent search, personalized recommendations, and LLM-powered features.

I've also never been in charge of a team formally. My team dad approach has worked well for mentoring and informal leadership - I've successfully mentored 3 backend developers transitioning to full-stack roles. But I want to develop formal leadership capabilities - managing team dynamics, making strategic decisions, coordinating multiple people toward shared goals. Whether I'm leading web developers, AI engineers, or robotics teams in the future, these capabilities will be valuable.
