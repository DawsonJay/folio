# How did you transition from art to tech?

Coding gave me what art couldn't - bringing ideas into reality with clear, objective parameters for success. Code either works or it doesn't. That clarity drew me in.

I completed a Foundation Year in Illustration at the University of Derby but realized fine art was too dependent on mood and public opinion to be sustainable. I struggled with how little it exercised my intellectual side. I'm both creative and logical. Art exercised one, tech exercises both.

I started teaching myself programming around 2014, well before considering it as a career. I began with Java because I wanted to understand AI concepts. I didn't follow structured tutorials - I read books, tried things, built projects. Exploratory approach that's still how I learn today.

I tried formal education at Heriot-Watt University for AI but found it too slow. I'd already been coding for years and the coursework held me back. I completed 2 years, earned a Certificate of Merit, but left for a more practical path. The Ironhack bootcamp in May 2020 bridged personal projects into professional skills - React, Node.js, MongoDB, professional practices. Within months, I secured my first role as Junior Web Developer at BriefYourMarket.

Art gave me valuable instincts in tech. Gut sense for when code structure feels wrong or when project architecture will produce bugs later. Knowing when something is done - when it's stable and functional and adding more is perfectionism rather than professionalism. The Integrations Dashboard has run 3+ years without maintenance. I approach UI design understanding visual balance, color theory, and how people interact with information.

Art and technology are different expressions of the same drive - taking ideas and making them real in the world.
