# Where does maintenance end and improvement begin for you?

Maintenance keeps something stable — no bugs, no structural drift that will unravel later. Improvement moves it toward a better state. The line is often about what's actually at risk. If a codebase is showing early stress signals — minor bugs pointing to structural weakness, components harder to extend than they should be — that's critical work disguised as cosmetic upkeep, not optional housekeeping.

The trap is treating visible improvement as the only kind. I think of neglected internals like a stage prop: it looks real from the front but falls apart on the slightest touch. A product has to hold up over time, not just in a demo.

On Nexus I addressed structural problems as they emerged, which kept the codebase flexible and let features expand quickly without significant rework. Build at BriefYourMarket — my first employer — showed the other side: contractors built features fast without structural stake, and by the time I worked in it, meaningful improvement wasn't on the table without something close to a rebuild. Maintenance there meant surgical changes — smallest possible fix, smallest possible footprint. Not making it worse was about the best you could hope for.
