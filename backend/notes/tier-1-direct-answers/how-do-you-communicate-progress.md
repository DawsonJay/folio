# How do you communicate progress on your work?

Daily standups make progress communication relatively easy — the same people are hearing the thread evolve day by day, so context accumulates and explanations stay short. The harder version is when updates are infrequent, the audience has a different technical background, and a lot has happened since the last check-in.

The clearest example was communicating progress on the Nexus Dashboard to the head developer at Nurtur. Updates were irregular, development was moving fast, and he was primarily a backend developer while my work was almost entirely frontend architecture. Giving him a React-level walkthrough wouldn't have helped either of us. Instead I talked about the project the same way I later talked about it to the heads of departments: what I'd built, what it could do, how the structure made future additions fast and cheap. Metaphors over mechanisms — components like Lego blocks, graphs like trading cards you could swap in and out of the library.

The underlying principle is the same regardless of audience: focus on what changed, what it means, and what it enables next. Technical detail only if it's directly relevant to a decision they need to make.
