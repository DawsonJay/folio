# How do you handle a request from a teammate that isn't technically viable on the frontend?

It's almost never adversarial — it's a communication problem. If I don't know exactly why something won't work I investigate first so I can come back with precise reasons rather than a vague no. People accept a clear explanation; vague refusals breed resentment between teams.

A solid example was on Nexus. The sales team suggested regex filtering for a series of entries — it sounded simple but involved significant frontend and backend work. Rather than refusing in the meeting I said I'd look into it and get back to them. I talked to Craig (backend), mapped what was needed on both sides, and came back with a clear picture: what was feasible, what wasn't, and what alternatives would get them similar value. We then talked through which options mattered most to them.

Non-technical people asking for things usually have good ideas — they're working with unfamiliar constraints, not making unreasonable demands. Explaining those constraints clearly so the next request is better-grounded is the sensible way forward. I want them to keep suggesting things. That feedback loop was constant on Nexus: proactively connect stakeholders and engineering, align what's valuable with what's actually feasible, feed clear trade-offs back.
