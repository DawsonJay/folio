# What do you enjoy about working with specialists in fields very different from yours?

Learning is a major driver for me. I actively requested new experiences at Nurtur and built a range of perspectives that made it easier to be useful in unfamiliar contexts — thinking like a backend developer or a frontend developer, or like my technophobic parents, or like friends who grew up with computers. That range is what lets me communicate between technical and non-technical teams and build interfaces that actually bridge the gap.

The most memorable example isn't professional: helping a technophobic friend set up Discord. Something I thought trivial took hours and visibly stressed her. It made me realise that for a lot of people friction is genuinely upsetting — any friction is enough to make them quit. Up until that point I'd unconsciously been designing for developers. After that I started designing for technophobes: if something feels frictionless to someone who finds technology stressful, it's excellent for everyone else.

Working between the Integrations backend team and sales had a similar effect. The engineer-first dashboard got the data visible, which was the right early priority — but it didn't answer the questions a salesperson arrived at a page with. That contrast taught me what good design actually is.
