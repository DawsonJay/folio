# Have you ever been the only domain expert on a team?

The clearest examples are Integrations and Nexus — I was the only frontend developer on each for the entire project. On Nexus it was me and Craig, who handled the backend; even the overseeing developer was backend-only. I was responsible for everything: React, Material UI, CSS, architecture decisions, and design.

There were people I could escalate to when something was seriously wrong, but they weren't in the codebase day-to-day — getting a second opinion meant someone putting in real effort to understand code they didn't know. It wasn't a casual lean-over-and-ask. Over time this gave me a reputation as highly mobile: I regularly picked up frontend tasks that didn't justify a dedicated team, and backend developers would come to me informally with frontend questions.

The freedom was genuinely good — I worked the way I thought best. The harder part was learning mostly from my own mistakes rather than a peer reviewing the same code. I missed being able to share a clever solution with someone who'd actually understand what I'd done.
