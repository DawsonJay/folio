# Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker

I've never had a major disagreement because I tend to catch them early. My starting assumption is that we both want the same thing — a project that works — and that any disagreement is usually a gap in shared information, not a genuine conflict of goals.

When I disagree with someone, I listen first and try to understand what they know that I don't, what they're actually worried about. Then I explain my perspective in terms of their concerns rather than just reasserting my position. That exchange usually resolves things — what seemed like a conflict turns out to be two people with different pieces of the same picture.

When it doesn't resolve, I respect authority clearly. If the team lead disagrees with me after I've laid out everything I know, the decision is theirs — they own the responsibility for the outcome and I'll execute professionally. If the disagreement is between peers in an area where I have more direct experience, I'll make the call, own it, and explain my reasoning clearly so the decision is transparent. The goal is to get to the right answer, not to win.
