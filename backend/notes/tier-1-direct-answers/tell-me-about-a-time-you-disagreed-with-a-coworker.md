# Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker

I've never had a major disagreement because I'm very good at managing them before they blow up into anything bigger. We all want the same thing in a team - to make a project that works well and gives value. Any disagreement after that is usually a lack of understanding or communication.

Most disagreements come from miscommunication or different perspectives on the same goal, not from fundamental conflicts. When everyone wants the project to succeed, disagreements are usually solvable through better understanding.

I always listen to the other person's view and experience, and then give my own, and that's always cleared things up. Most of the time, when people understand each other's perspectives and reasoning, the disagreement resolves itself. What seemed like a conflict was actually just different information or different experiences leading to different conclusions.

When I listen first, I'm trying to understand: What problem are they trying to solve? What information do they have that I don't? What experiences led them to their conclusion? What are they actually concerned about?

Then when I explain my perspective, I'm connecting it to their concerns. I'm showing how my approach addresses what they care about, or explaining why I think a different approach is better given the constraints we both understand.

Sometimes there's still a disagreement after we've both listened and explained. Then it comes down to authority. If I think something should be done a certain way and the team lead disagrees, as long as I've given them all the relevant information, the decision is theirs and I'll accept it because they own the project and the responsibility for it.

When we both have the same authority, I'll still listen, but if it's an area where I have more experience, I'll go forward with my own approach and they'll accept it. It's about recognizing expertise. If I've built similar systems before and understand the tradeoffs, and someone else hasn't, it makes sense for me to lead in that area. They'll accept it because they trust my expertise, just as I accept their decisions when they have ownership or more relevant experience.
