# What makes you unique as a developer?

What makes me unique is the combination of artistic intuition and technical rigor. My art background gives me intuition and flexibility that complement my technical skills. I can often tell through gut instinct when a solution is wrong or when the structure of a project is flawed and will produce bugs later on. This intuition helps me catch issues early, before they become bigger problems.

As a coder, you get taught to rely purely on logic - it's seen as a very logical intellectual pursuit and in some ways it is, but it's also a creative endeavor. Being able to rely on accurate feelings means I can be sensitive to the underlying structure of the code, to be able to intuit when I'm on the wrong path and I need to fix the current problem in a new way. It means that I can follow an instinct on what might be causing a bug and usually be right. For investigations and awareness of the code on a more abstract, structural level, intuition is one of my most valuable skills. I always follow up an intuition with a rigorous logical investigation - the feeling acts as a compass, not as proof.

It helps with knowing when something is done because when working on a painting, or throwing a pot, or taking a photo, there's a point, a sudden gut feeling when you know things are exactly right, that anything else you do will make things worse or be wasted effort. That's a skill it takes years to develop, and my artistic background did that for me. It's very useful to know when a piece of code is done, where it's stable and functional and well written, and adding any more polish is perfectionism as opposed to being practical.

My art background also helps with the idea of failing forward. Art doesn't come with a plan or a set path - you have a vision and feel your way toward it, where failures become creative solutions. When I hit a technical constraint or discover my initial approach won't work, I don't get discouraged. I pivot, I adapt, I find creative solutions that sometimes end up better than my original plan. I see setbacks as part of the process and usually turn them into something valuable.

My leadership style makes me unique too. I found during my time teaching kayaking that most people generally want the same things. In kayaking, students wanted to become better kayakers. In work contexts, people want to make good products. In both situations, people want to be valued, heard, and supported in their growth. I don't think people need to be forced or manipulated to do good work - they just need to be in an environment that lets them do that.

The breadth of my background - art, teaching, kayaking, self-taught coding, professional development - gives me perspectives that pure computer science graduates might not have. I understand how people learn, how to communicate across different contexts, how to see problems from multiple angles, and how to find creative solutions when standard approaches don't work.

My approach to failure is different too. I see project cancellations or bugs as learning opportunities. Jam Hot didn't work out, but it taught me about dataset quality. Cirrus was cancelled, but it taught me about project scoping. The hardest bug in WhatNow taught me about measuring the right things. This ability to extract value from setbacks makes me resilient and continuously improving.

The combination of these qualities - artistic intuition, teaching experience, creative problem-solving, team-focused leadership, and technical depth - creates a developer profile that's genuinely unique. I'm not just a coder, and I'm not just a creative person. I'm someone who brings both together in ways that make teams stronger and projects better.
