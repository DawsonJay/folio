# How did you get into development?

I started in art — a Foundation Year in Illustration at the University of Derby. I loved the creative work but found it didn't satisfy the intellectual side of me. When I left, I started teaching myself to code around 2014, beginning with Java because I wanted to understand AI. No structured courses, just books and building half-formed things until they worked. That exploratory approach is still how I learn.

I tried formal education at Heriot-Watt University on an AI programme, but found the gap between coursework and real-world development too wide. I'd already been coding for years and the pace felt like it was holding me back. I left after two years — I had a Certificate of Merit for the first year — and did an Ironhack bootcamp in May 2020 instead. That was a deliberate choice to bridge self-taught skills into professional ones: React, Node.js, version control, working in teams under real constraints. Within a few months I had my first professional role as a Junior Web Developer at BriefYourMarket.

What drew me to development was that it satisfies both the creative and the analytical. Code either works or it doesn't. That objectivity is what art lacked for me, and what I've found deeply satisfying ever since.
