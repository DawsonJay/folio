# How do you use AI in your development workflow?

Working heavily with AI tools hardened my solo coding discipline more than anything else. AI makes sweeping changes without full context — it'll refactor broadly without understanding the architectural intent behind what it's touching. To stay on top of that I developed tight habits: small modular components where changes only affect one contained area, careful review of everything AI produced, and tests around core mechanics to catch quiet drift from what the code was supposed to do. Those habits began as a response to AI, but they're general now — AI was just the chaotic, high-pressure force that made them non-negotiable.

The other thing I built was a documentation layer designed for AI to query efficiently. On Nexus I keep a running dev log and distil it periodically into a vault of atomic notes — structured, concise, purpose-built for context. When I bring AI back into an area, it reads the notes rather than re-deriving everything from raw code each time. That keeps the cost down, the context richer, and the suggestions more accurate. The same notes capture recurring bugs and their fixes — things invisible to a public search engine but expensive to rediscover without a record.

The net effect is that working well with AI looks like working with a capable but context-starved colleague: brief it clearly, review what it produces, and build systems that make it cheaper and more reliable to use over time.
