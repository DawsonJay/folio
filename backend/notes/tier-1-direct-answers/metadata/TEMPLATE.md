## Format Guidelines

### The STANDALONE + CURIOSITY Framework

Each answer MUST accomplish four things independently:

1. **Establish Credibility** (Who am I and why should you care?)
   - Lead with concrete achievements or expertise
   - Include metrics, impact, or scope when relevant
   - Avoid weak openings like "I'm a developer seeking..."

2. **Answer the Specific Question** with concrete value
   - Provide specific examples, not generalizations
   - Include relevant dates, project names, technologies
   - Show both breadth and depth where appropriate

3. **Create Curiosity** (Make them want to ask follow-ups)
   - Mention interesting projects/challenges without explaining everything
   - Tease depth of knowledge without drowning in details
   - Show growth trajectory or future direction

4. **Forward Momentum** (Connect to what you want)
   - Brief connection to career goals or role fit
   - Demonstrate you're growing, not static

### Answer Length by Question Tier

**Tier 1: High-Probability Questions** (200-250 words)
- "Tell me about yourself", "Why should we hire you", "What are you looking for"
- These must be PERFECT standalone answers
- Lead with strongest value proposition
- Multiple curiosity hooks
- Target: 60-90 seconds spoken (~200-250 words written)

**Tier 2: Technical Questions** (150-200 words)
- "Tell me about your React experience", "What AI/ML projects have you built"
- Establish credibility quickly (first sentence)
- One standout example with specifics
- Show depth without losing non-technical readers
- Target: 45-60 seconds spoken (~150-200 words written)

**Tier 3: Behavioral/Specific Questions** (100-150 words)
- "Tell me about a mistake", "What databases have you used"
- Brief context + specific story/example + lesson/value
- Quick credibility anchor
- Connect back to broader capabilities
- Target: 30-45 seconds spoken (~100-150 words written)

### Style Guidelines - CRITICAL TONE RULES

**GOLDEN RULE: Let facts speak for themselves. Never tell the reader what to conclude.**

❌ **NEVER use prescriptive language:**
- "This demonstrates..." / "This proves..." / "This shows..."
- "My superpower is..." / "The proof is in..."
- "What makes me unique is..." / "What sets me apart..."
- Any phrase that tells the reader what they should think

✅ **INSTEAD: State facts directly:**
- ❌ "This demonstrates I can build reliable systems"
- ✅ "Dashboard running 3+ years without maintenance"
- ❌ "My superpower is building interfaces that last"  
- ✅ "I build interfaces that last. Dashboard running 3+ years without bug reports"
- ❌ "What makes me different is my AI focus"
- ✅ "I build AI-powered web applications alongside traditional frontend work"

**Voice & Format:**
- **Voice**: First person ("I", "my", "me") - answer as James
- **Format**: Plain text only - NO markdown formatting (no **, *, _, -, numbered lists, links in text)
- **Tone**: Direct, factual, authentic - no marketing speak or buzzwords
- **Mobile-First**: Assume readers are on phones - tight, scannable, impactful

**Authentic Language:**
- ❌ Avoid: "positioning myself", "cutting edge", "innovative solutions", "leverage", "synergy"
- ✅ Use: Natural, conversational language - how you'd actually talk
- ✅ Be specific: Actual project names, real metrics, concrete technologies

### Strategic Redundancy

Since each answer must standalone, some repetition across files is necessary BUT:

✅ **DO repeat:**
- Core value proposition (vary the wording)
- Flagship projects (highlight different angles per answer)
- Key achievements (in relevant contexts)

❌ **DON'T repeat:**
- Verbatim sentences across files
- Same project angle multiple times
- Generic filler that doesn't answer the question

**Example - Same project, different angles:**
- Frontend experience: "...dashboard that's run 3+ years without maintenance - proof I build UIs that last"
- Code quality: "...3+ years with zero bug reports demonstrates my focus on clean architecture and testing"
- Successful project: "...succeeded by focusing on real user needs over fancy features"

### Emotion Options
Choose one: `happy`, `thinking`, `surprised`, `derp`, `tired`, `annoyed`
- **happy**: Positive topics, achievements, enthusiasm
- **thinking**: Technical discussions, problem-solving
- **surprised**: Impressive achievements, unexpected outcomes
- **derp**: Limitations, things you don't know
- **tired**: Long processes, repetitive topics
- **annoyed**: Frustrations, negative experiences (use sparingly)

### shortTitle (optional field)

Only add `**shortTitle:**` when the `# Title` exceeds 45 characters. The shortTitle must:
- Still be a well-formed question (it is the exact text submitted when a user clicks the suggestion chip)
- Be a shorter rephrasing of the same question, not a label or summary
- Be 45 characters or fewer

Example:
- Title: `# Tell me about a time you had to meet a tight deadline` (53 chars)
- shortTitle: `How do you handle tight deadlines?` (34 chars ✓)

When shortTitle is absent, the full title is used as the display text.

## GOLD STANDARD Example: "Tell me about yourself"

This is the template for excellence - every answer should meet this standard:

# Tell me about yourself

I'm a Frontend Engineer with 5.5 years of professional experience, specializing in React/TypeScript and AI integration. I build interfaces that last. The Integrations Dashboard I shipped at Nurtur has run 3+ years without maintenance or bug reports. The Nexus Dashboard I worked on went from 15+ second load times to under 5 seconds through strategic loading patterns and caching.

I spent 3.5 years at Nurtur - a real estate tech company with about 20 developers, working in small focused teams of 3-5. I built full-stack applications with strongest expertise in frontend. I led technical architecture for internal dashboards managing distributed job systems, made independent decisions on data fetching and caching strategies, and mentored 3 backend developers transitioning to full-stack roles. The team was restructured in February 2026, so I'm seeking my next opportunity.

I build AI-powered web applications alongside traditional frontend work. I've deployed recommendation systems using machine learning, LLM integrations for language learning, and semantic search chatbots. These aren't side projects - they're production applications solving real problems. I'm working at the intersection of React development and AI capabilities.

I transitioned into tech from art about 11 years ago. Started self-taught in 2014, did an Ironhack bootcamp in 2020, and have been working professionally since. The art background helps with interface design and architectural intuition - I can sense structural problems early. I bridge gaps between technical and non-technical teams and focus on building things that create real value.

I'm looking for mid to senior frontend roles where I can integrate AI capabilities into products that matter. Available immediately.

---

**Why this works:**
- ✅ **250 words** - Perfect Tier 1 length
- ✅ **Facts speak** - No "demonstrates" or "proves", just direct statements
- ✅ **Credibility first** - Opens with concrete metrics
- ✅ **Context provided** - Company size, team structure, availability explained
- ✅ **Career path clear** - 11 years total timeline addresses commitment question
- ✅ **Production work** - AI projects clarified as real, not side projects
- ✅ **Curiosity hooks** - Projects mentioned without full explanation
- ✅ **Recruiter-friendly** - Addresses all concerns, no red flags, scannable on mobile

## Address Recruiter Concerns (Especially for Tier 1 questions)

Recruiters scan for red flags. Proactively address concerns without dwelling on them:

**✅ Include when relevant:**
- **Company context**: Size, industry, team structure (helps them understand scale of work)
  - "real estate tech company with about 20 developers, working in small focused teams of 3-5"
- **Availability explanation**: Brief, factual, not defensive
  - "The team was restructured in February 2026" (not "I was made redundant" or long explanations)
- **Career path clarity**: Timeline that makes sense
  - "transitioned into tech from art about 11 years ago...working professionally since" (shows commitment)
- **Production vs side projects**: Make it clear AI work is real
  - "These aren't side projects - they're production applications solving real problems"

**❌ Don't:**
- Dwell on negatives or make excuses
- Over-explain employment gaps
- Sound defensive about career changes
- Leave obvious questions unanswered

**Common errors to watch for:**
- ❌ Project dates (moh-ami built in ONE DAY, not "3-4 weeks")
- ❌ Number precision (1,249 activities in WhatNow, not "1,250")
- ❌ Timeline accuracy (3.5 years at Nurtur: July 2022 - February 2026)
- ❌ Team sizes (usually 3-5 people at Nurtur)
- ❌ Proficiency claims that aren't backed by foundational notes

**If unsure about a fact, DON'T GUESS - check the source material.**
