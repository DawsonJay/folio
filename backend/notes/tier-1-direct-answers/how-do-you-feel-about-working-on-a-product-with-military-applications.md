# How do you feel about working on a product with military applications?

My position is case-dependent with clear red lines.

For a military not committing atrocities — even one involved in what I'd consider a just or defensive war — I'm okay with that. It's not categorically different from paying taxes that support a military. Militaries aren't black and white; the world is too complicated to say war and armed forces are always wrong.

My red lines are about specific conduct: genocide, torture, mass civilian casualties. If a company's product was supporting a government committing that kind of crime I would refuse the work — not just raise it internally, but refuse. Choosing to supply one side in a conflict with that record is a major ethical step that flags and internal escalation alone don't address.

When I object to a regime it's about conduct and material complicity, not the ethnicity or background of anyone I work with. Proportionality matters — there's a significant gap between a colleague's cultural identity and materially enabling serious wrongdoing. These hard lines are extreme and difficult to cross in most software work. I won't campaign in the office or have loud political discussions, but I wouldn't stay at a company whose actions crossed those kinds of rare, material lines.
