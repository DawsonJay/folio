# How do you know when something is done?

Done means three things to me: it meets the specification, it's structurally stable — unlikely to develop serious bugs over time — and it's passed the relevant checks, whether that's peer review, tests, or QA. That's become as much instinct as checklist.

The harder question is "could be better". The answer is always yes. Before I start anything I form a clear picture of its purpose and what it needs to do. From there, additional work past a certain point stops serving the purpose and just adds complexity. My art background is part of why that clicks — there's a recognisable moment in a painting where adding more moves it away from what it's communicating rather than toward it.

On WhatNow I shipped earlier than I'd have liked. The internals were solid and the code was clean, but I was building portfolio projects under time pressure and I didn't have the time to make the interface polished. The AI-generated interface meant I sacrificed something I care about — design — to protect what mattered more: a codebase that was stable and would hold up. Knowing what to sacrifice is part of knowing when something is done.
