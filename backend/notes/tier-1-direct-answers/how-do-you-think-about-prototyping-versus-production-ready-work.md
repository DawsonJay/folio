# How do you think about prototyping versus production-ready work?

A prototype has a purpose: testing a theory, showing a stakeholder what something could look like, or exploring a direction quickly. That's a valid and sometimes the right approach. The trap is treating it as something it isn't.

I think of it like a stage prop — it looks real from the front, but behind it is cardboard and duct tape that'll fall over with the slightest change. The problem isn't building the prop; the problem is when stakeholders see a polished surface and assume it's finished. They can't see the internals. If you've built something exploratory, being explicit about that upfront — this is a prototype to test the idea, not production-ready — is what makes the pattern work. Without that communication you end up with debt everyone's surprised by.

When I do build something disposable I try to make it modular where I can, so you can swap out internals and replace them with something robust later rather than discarding everything. That pattern works — it's just harder to pull off than building it right from the start, and it only holds up if both sides are clear on what's being built and why.

The instinct I work against is building the stage prop permanently. Solid internals and a prototype surface aren't mutually exclusive; often you can build the right structure early and still iterate quickly on the visible layer.
