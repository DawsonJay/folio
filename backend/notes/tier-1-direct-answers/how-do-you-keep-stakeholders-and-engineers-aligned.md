# How do you keep stakeholders and engineers aligned?

The most common failure mode I've seen is the gap between what stakeholders think is possible and what engineers think is worth building — and nobody closing it. On Nexus I drove that loop consistently rather than waiting for it to exist.

The sales team had domain knowledge I didn't have: they knew what would make them more effective in their work. But they couldn't always translate that into frontend terms, and I couldn't translate frontend reality into their priorities without actually talking to them. So after each round of suggestions from them, I'd investigate on my side — mapping what the frontend would need, checking with Craig (the backend developer) on what was needed on his — then come back with a clear picture: what was feasible, what wasn't, and what alternatives might get them similar value. Then I asked which trade-offs mattered most to them.

That loop produced better suggestions each time. The point wasn't to say yes or no — it was to give them enough grounding in how the system worked that the next idea arrived better-shaped. It also meant I learned what was actually valuable to them, which changed how I thought about what to build next.

It wasn't a process handed to me. It was something I saw was needed and built into how the project ran.
