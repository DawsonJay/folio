# Tell me about collaborating with non-technical people

The Integrations Dashboard at Nurtur required bridging two teams who barely spoke the same language. Sales: spreadsheets, customer conversations. Backend: Azure functions, C# code. Both needed to collaborate on the same product.

My approach: focus on what people need to accomplish, not how the code works. With sales, I framed technical constraints in terms of their workflow and customer impact. With the backend team, I translated sales requests into data structures and API requirements. I was the interface between them.

I listen before I explain. In cross-team work, what people think they need and what they actually need are often different. Listening first means I'm solving the real problem.

I use metaphors and create psychological safety. The biggest barrier isn't complexity — it's people feeling like they'll never understand. I relate technical concepts to things they already know, and I let them know we all started where they are. That openness is what makes actual learning happen.

Background helps: art, teaching kayaking, bridging teams. I've always moved between technical and non-technical contexts. Good communication isn't showing what you know — it's helping others understand what they need to.
