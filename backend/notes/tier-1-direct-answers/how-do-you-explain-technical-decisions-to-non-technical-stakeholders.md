# How do you explain technical decisions to non-technical stakeholders?

Through metaphor, mostly. Technical concepts are rarely as unique as they seem — there are usually familiar equivalents in everyday life, and those connections are what make explanations stick.

Refactoring is a good example. It's hard to explain why something "fixed" isn't actually fixed if the underlying problem was just patched over. The real answer involves system architecture and why symptoms reappear, which glazes eyes quickly. But "we put a bucket under a leak — the floor's dry, but the roof is still the problem, and it's cheaper to replace a tile now than the whole roof later" lands immediately. The idea is identical; the framing makes the difference.

The clearest test of that was a full presentation to a room of heads of departments at Nurtur about the Nexus Dashboard — sales leads, IT managers, senior management, none of them React developers. I didn't talk about state management or component architecture. I talked about the system being built from Lego blocks: snap pieces together to make a new page in minutes, not days. Graphs built like trading cards — easy to swap in, and we keep the spares in a library. I walked through the documentation I'd written, highlighted the features they had specifically requested, and explained why the structure would make future additions cheap. The reception was strong, and the questions were engaged rather than polite.
