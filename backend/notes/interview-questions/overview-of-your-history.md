## Question

Give me an overview of your history.

## Answer

I'm an experienced full‑stack engineer who likes to own product features end‑to‑end and make teams more effective.

I came into tech from an arts and design background, which is why I care a lot about storytelling and UX, and how products actually feel to the user. Around 2014 I started teaching myself to code, then went through a bootcamp to formalize things and moved into professional engineering roles. Since then I've mainly been building product‑focused web applications, and working in environments where good communication and reliability really matter.

Most recently I worked as a full‑stack engineer at Nurtur on a small team. On the front‑end I led React/TypeScript work on major surfaces like our integrations dashboard and reporting tools: taking fuzzy requirements, shaping user journeys, and designing the architecture. On the backend I worked with RESTful APIs and sometimes dug into a decades‑old C# codebase to track down tricky issues that surfaced in the UI. A big focus for me there was long‑term stability and performance, as well as making the codebase friendlier for other developers through clearer patterns and better communication. I also spent time mentoring backend‑leaning teammates on front‑end practices and using metaphors and diagrams to align technical and non‑technical stakeholders.

Outside of work, my project work really ramped up alongside the recent AI boom. I've built things like a self‑teaching recommendation system and an AI‑powered portfolio assistant, where I combined modern front‑end stacks with LLMs and retrieval‑based architectures to ship real products quickly and then refine them based on usage. My pattern is usually: pick a concrete product goal, learn whatever technology is needed to achieve it, and then tidy up the system so it's understandable and maintainable.

Going forward, I'm looking for roles where I can keep that trajectory—owning meaningful product areas across the stack, collaborating closely with product and engineering, and taking on more architectural and leadership responsibilities.
