# Cross-Functional Communication — Working Directly with the Sales Team

On both the Integrations Dashboard and the Nexus Dashboard, I worked in direct communication with the sales team throughout the build — not just handing off a finished product, but involving them in the design process and getting feedback at each stage.

## Integrations Dashboard

The Integrations Dashboard was built for the sales team's daily use. I collaborated directly with them on requirements and design, understanding how they worked and what would actually make their jobs easier rather than just building what was specified. The product was released in stages, which meant I could watch how their working lives changed as each piece shipped. That feedback loop — seeing real users adapt to and benefit from what I built — was what made the work feel like it mattered.

The users themselves expressed gratitude directly. That kind of direct signal from the people using your work is rare and it shaped how I approach products built for internal teams.

## Nexus Dashboard

On Nexus, I was in direct communication with the sales team throughout the design phase. The dashboard was for managing distributed job processing infrastructure — technically complex on the backend — but what mattered to the sales team was clarity and visibility. Understanding their mental model, not just the technical requirements, informed every architectural decision: the hierarchical drill-down navigation, the visual emphasis on patterns like backlogs and spikes, the way data was layered so you could see what mattered immediately without being overwhelmed.

## What This Demonstrates

Working directly with end users — in this case a non-technical sales team — across the full lifecycle of two projects gave me a practical understanding of cross-functional communication. I was the interface between backend systems and the people using the output. I translated technical constraints into product decisions, and translated user needs back into data structures and API contracts.

The ability to do that — to hold the technical and user-facing perspectives at the same time — is part of why both projects succeeded and why the Integrations Dashboard has required zero maintenance since launch.
