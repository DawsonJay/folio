# Ethics and Red Lines: Military Applications, Uncomfortable Features, and Where I Stand

My position on ethically complicated work is case-dependent with clear, non-negotiable red lines. I don't have a blanket refusal for defence or dual-use work, and I don't have a blanket acceptance either.

For a military not committing atrocities — even one involved in a conflict I'd consider just or defensive — I'm comfortable with that work. I don't see it as categorically different from paying taxes that fund a military.

My red lines are about specific conduct: genocide, torture, mass civilian casualties. If a company's product was materially supporting a government committing those crimes, I would refuse the work — not just flag it internally and carry on, but refuse. When I object to a regime it's about conduct and material complicity, not the ethnicity or background of anyone I work with. Proportionality matters: there's a massive gap between harmless everyday things and materially enabling serious wrongdoing. I'm only talking about the latter.

For uncomfortable features that arise during normal work, I follow a clear escalation path: first check with a trusted colleague to make sure I'm not seeing something that isn't there; then raise it with my manager with a proposed solution; if refused, escalate higher; if that fails, resign. I won't sabotage the feature or compromise on it. I have a contract and violating it would go against my ethics as strongly as the original concern. If I'm responsible for shipping, I ship — even while escalating. Removing myself at the critical moment would be the same as sabotage to me.

Before I had professional experience I ended an interview politely when a game studio revealed it was for a freemium game. I'm against freemium games because they use slot machine mechanics that extract money from a small number of people who can't afford it. That was an early version of the same pattern: clear position, proportionate response, no drama.

These positions are reserved for genuinely serious situations — not everyday business decisions I happen to disagree with.
