# My Ideal Work Environment

## What Works Best

The working situation I've found most effective is a small setup — three or four people — where I have real autonomy over my area, a clear point of accountability, and at least one other person to collaborate closely with.

The closest I've had to this professionally was the Nexus Dashboard at Nurtur: just me and one backend developer, the frontend design entirely mine, structured so I could present progress to the head developer for approval. That combination of ownership, collaboration, and accountability produced some of my best work.

## Seeing the Impact

What makes work feel genuinely good is being close enough to the impact to see it. On both Nexus and the Integrations Dashboard, I was in direct communication with the sales team throughout — they were excited about what we were building, and with the Integrations Dashboard I got to watch their working lives get easier as it released in stages.

That feedback loop — real users, real change, visible improvement — is what inspires me to make something excellent rather than just functional. I work better when I can see who I'm building for.

## Trust and Accountability

I need trust to do my best work — specifically, the freedom to design and build something the way I think it should be done, with someone whose judgment I respect checking it at the right moments. That's different from being left alone, and different from being closely supervised. It's the middle ground where good work happens.

## Shared Mission

I work best alongside people who care about the outcome the same way I do — inspired by the work, not just employed by it. The attitude of a small team has real impact on how the whole thing feels, and that means my attitude does too. I put effort into making the environment better and it shows in small teams in a way that gets lost in larger organisations.

## Creating the Environment

I've also learned that the right environment doesn't always arrive fully formed. Being someone who raises problems directly, mentors teammates, and cares about how the team functions is part of how you build it. I'm not waiting for ideal conditions — I'm part of creating them.

## What Doesn't Work

- **Isolation**: Freelancing taught me I don't want to work alone. I need people to share the work with — not just collaborate functionally, but to show a clever solution to someone who understands why it's elegant.
- **Meaningless work**: I struggle with work I know doesn't matter to anyone. I find it hard to care about code I know will cause problems next month and that nobody will notice either way.
- **Micromanagement disguised as support**: Close supervision without trust undermines the quality of the work. I need the expectation to be clear and then the space to meet it.
