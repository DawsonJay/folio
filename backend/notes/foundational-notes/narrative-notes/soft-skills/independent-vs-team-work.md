# Independent vs Team Work: Why I Prefer Teams

## What I Learned from Freelancing

After my first professional role, I spent 15 months as a freelance web developer. This period taught me valuable lessons about what I need in my work environment and what kind of work structure suits me best. Freelancing taught me that I need teammates to share solutions with, people to discuss technical challenges with, and the ability to support others' growth.

I took on freelance work because I wanted independence and the opportunity to work on diverse projects. I built websites for small businesses, developed web applications for clients, and handled everything from initial consultation through deployment. The work gave me experience in customer relations, business development, project management, and handling ambiguous requirements.

What I discovered was that while I enjoyed being responsible for my own projects, I prefer working in team environments. The isolation of freelancing made me realize I need collaborative environments to thrive. I'm more motivated when I can show someone a clever approach and they understand why it's elegant. The joy of solving a problem is amplified when I can share it with someone who appreciates the solution.

## What Teams Provide

Having a team gives a spread of experience, allows me to focus on one aspect at a time, or manage the project as a whole not both, and provides a buffer between me and the general public. As I've mentioned, it's also important to me to be able to support the people around me, and working alone took that away.

**Spread of experience**: On a team, different people bring different expertise. I don't have to be good at everything - I can focus on what I'm strong at and trust teammates to handle areas where they have more experience.

**Focus**: I can focus on one aspect of a project - like frontend development or system architecture - while others handle other aspects. Or I can manage the project as a whole while others handle implementation. I don't have to do both simultaneously.

**Buffer from clients**: On a team, there's usually a project manager or someone else who handles client communication. I can focus on the technical work without managing client expectations, scope changes, and vague requirements directly.

**Supporting others**: One of the things I value most is being able to help teammates grow and succeed. Working alone took that away. I need to be able to support the people around me - that's part of what makes work meaningful for me.

## The Ideal Balance

I prefer working in small teams (3-4 people) where I can have focused collaboration but also work independently when needed. I can do deep work on my own tasks, then come together with the team to coordinate, share solutions, and get feedback.

The key is having the option to work independently when that's most effective, but always having teammates available for collaboration, support, and shared problem-solving. I don't want constant collaboration - that's exhausting. But I also don't want complete isolation - that's draining in a different way.

## What Freelancing Clarified

Freelancing taught me that I need:
- **Teammates to share solutions with**: I'm more motivated when I can show someone a clever approach and they understand why it's elegant
- **People to share difficult challenges with**: Problems are easier when you can discuss them with teammates
- **Supporting others**: I need to be able to help people grow - that's part of what makes work meaningful
- **Focus on strengths**: I want to focus on what I'm good at, not handle every aspect of every project
- **Buffer from client management**: I want to do technical work, not manage client expectations and scope changes

These needs are met in team environments, not in solo freelancing. That's why I prefer teams - not because I can't work independently, but because teams provide the collaboration, support, and focus that make work sustainable and meaningful.

## The Bottom Line

I can work independently when needed. I'm comfortable with self-direction and I can handle solo projects. But I prefer teams because they provide collaboration, support, focus, and the ability to help others grow. These things make work better for me, and I think they make my work better too.

The ideal is a small team where I can do focused independent work but always have teammates available for collaboration, problem-solving, and mutual support. That's the balance that works for me.
