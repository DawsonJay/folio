# Handling Stress and Pressure

I've developed strong stress management skills through kayaking slalom competition and high-pressure technical work.

## Competition Experience

As a kayaking slalom competitor, I learned to handle tremendous pressure. Slalom is a sport where you paddle a kayak down a river through a series of gates as fast as possible. It takes great technical skill and strength, but it also takes certain useful character traits to do well. To compete in a competition, you have to handle a tremendous amount of pressure. You have to be able to move on from mistakes quickly - missing a gate adds to your final time, and it's easy to get upset over that mistake and miss more gates. You have to spend relentless hours training by yourself. You have to have a goal and work towards it. You have to manage time and resources, not just because I didn't have a dedicated coach, but because I also had to juggle university at the same time. You have to be willing to get up on frozen mornings and be willing to train on an icy river because missing a day is unacceptable to the goal.

Kayaking taught me a lot of lessons, but some of the most important are that the real work is done when no one is watching or pushing you, and about how to handle pressure and defeat. I didn't win every competition, but the ones I lost didn't hold me back. I learned that the only real failure is giving up.

## Pressure Management Strategies

Move on from mistakes quickly: In both kayaking and debugging, dwelling on errors wastes time. I acknowledge the mistake, learn from it, and refocus on the solution.

The real work is done when no one is watching: I learned that success comes from consistent effort in low-pressure moments, not just performance when it matters. This means staying calm under deadline pressure because I know the foundational work is solid.

Failure is giving up, not making mistakes: I didn't win every kayaking competition, but losses didn't hold me back. This mindset helps me stay resilient when projects face setbacks or technical challenges don't resolve quickly.

## Work Application

In technical work, I handle stress by: Trusting my intuition - Years of experience give me gut feelings about what's wrong, reducing panic when debugging complex issues. Breaking problems down - Frame the limits first, then find creative solutions within those constraints. Maintaining perspective - Remembering that I'm solving interesting problems with skilled teammates, not fighting fires alone. Taking breaks when needed - If I need to go hiking for 2 days with a notebook to think through a problem, that's how I work best.

I stay calm under pressure because I know consistent preparation and strong fundamentals mean I can handle whatever comes up. The Integrations Dashboard working for 3+ years without maintenance proves that thoughtful development prevents most problems before they happen, which reduces stress from the start.

## The Philosophy

Pressure is part of doing meaningful work. The key is building systems and processes that handle pressure gracefully, not avoiding it. I've learned to trust my preparation, stay focused on solutions rather than problems, and maintain perspective that most technical challenges are solvable with the right approach.
