# Biggest Weakness and Areas for Growth

## My Intensity: A Strength That Requires Balance

I have an intense focus that drives my best work - it's what lets me stay up until early morning getting one thing perfect, what creates things like the Integrations Dashboard, and what enables me to deliver exceptional results. This intensity is a strength, but like any strength, it requires balance to be sustainable.

When I get deeply invested in a project, I can become so absorbed that I need to actively manage my time and energy to maintain balance. This isn't a minor quirk - it's something I've learned to manage because sustainable excellence requires maintaining health, relationships, and perspective alongside technical work.

## How I Manage It

I've developed strategies to channel this intensity productively. I block out time for exercise and seeing friends as scheduled commitments, not optional activities. I create a stronger separation between my work and personal life. These aren't things I'll do "if I have time" - they're protected commitments that I treat with the same importance as critical work.

The key is recognizing that sustainable excellence requires balance. The best work comes from someone who's healthy, connected, and has perspective - not someone who's isolated and exhausted. I've learned that channeling intensity productively creates better outcomes than burning everything down to fuel one project.

## Learning the Lesson: The Ironhack Bootcamp

The Ironhack bootcamp taught me the importance of balance. I decided to focus on it entirely because I needed it to start my career - it became my absolute top priority. I put everything I had into it, and it worked - I completed it successfully and it launched my career. But I also learned that this level of intensity, while effective short-term, isn't sustainable long-term.

I learned that I needed to maintain other parts of my life even during intensive periods. This experience taught me to build balance into my approach from the start, rather than learning the lesson after the fact. Now I know how to channel that same intensity while maintaining the relationships, habits, and health that make sustained excellence possible.

## Areas I Want to Grow In

### Python and AI/ML Skills

I want to improve my skills and technologies relating to Python the most because I want to become more experienced in AI/ML and grow my talents there. I've used Python for AI/ML projects like WhatNow and moh-ami, but I want to deepen that expertise. This aligns with my long-term career goals of specializing in AI/ML integration for web applications.

### Leadership Abilities

I've taught people and I've controlled my own projects before, but I've never been in charge of a team and I would like to develop the skills to be good at that. My "team dad" approach has worked well for mentoring and informal leadership, but I want to develop formal leadership capabilities - managing team dynamics, making strategic decisions, coordinating multiple people toward shared goals.

Leadership skills transcend technology. Whether I'm leading web developers or AI engineers in the future, these capabilities will be valuable. I'll invest significant time developing them.

## The Growth Mindset

I don't see my intensity as something to eliminate - it's part of who I am and it drives my best work. Instead, I see it as something to manage and channel productively. The intensity that can be unsustainable when unchecked is the same intensity that creates excellent work when balanced.

Similarly, my growth areas aren't gaps to fill - they're opportunities to expand my impact. Python and AI/ML skills will let me work on more interesting problems. Leadership abilities will let me help teams accomplish more together than we could individually.

The goal isn't to become someone different. It's to become a better version of myself - someone who can channel intensity productively, who has deep technical skills in areas that matter, and who can lead teams to create things that wouldn't be possible alone.
