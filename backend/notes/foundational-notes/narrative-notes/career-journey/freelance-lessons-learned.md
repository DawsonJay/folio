# Freelance Experience: Learning What I Need

After my first professional role at BriefYourMarket.com ended in February 2021, I spent 15 months as a freelance web developer. This period taught me valuable lessons about what I need in my work environment and what kind of work structure suits me best.

I took on freelance work because I wanted independence and the opportunity to work on diverse projects. I built websites for small businesses, developed web applications for clients, and handled everything from initial consultation through deployment. The work ranged from simple brochure sites to more complex applications with databases and user authentication.

What I learned during this period was extensive. I gained experience in customer relations, business development, project management, and handling ambiguous requirements. Working independently meant I had to figure out everything myself - from finding clients to setting rates to managing expectations to delivering on time. These skills are valuable, but I discovered they weren't the skills I wanted to focus on.

The customer relations aspect was particularly challenging. I found that dealing with clients took significant time and energy away from the technical work I actually enjoyed. Explaining technical concepts to non-technical clients, managing expectations, handling scope changes, and navigating difficult conversations - these weren't activities that energized me. I realized I preferred working with technical teams where everyone understands the constraints and challenges.

The isolation of freelancing was another significant factor. Working alone meant I had no one to share clever solutions with, no one to discuss technical challenges with, no one to learn from through collaboration. I missed the collaborative environment where I could bounce ideas off teammates, get feedback on approaches, and work together to solve problems. The isolation made me realize I need collaborative environments to thrive.

The realization that sharing solutions matters as much as finding them was profound. I learned from freelancing that I need teammates to share solutions with. I'm more motivated when I can show someone a clever approach and they understand why it's elegant. The isolation made me realize that for me, the joy of solving a problem is amplified when I can share it with someone who appreciates the solution. This understanding has shaped my preference for team environments where collaboration and shared problem-solving are central to the work.

The financial reality was also sobering. Freelancing was more work (particularly work I didn't enjoy, like client relations) for less money than a salaried position. Being my own boss didn't compensate for the isolation and dealing with clients rather than focusing on technical excellence. I realized that the independence of freelancing wasn't worth the trade-offs for me.

One formative project was a **client online shop** I delivered **production-ready**: **React**, **TypeScript**, **Stripe**, **Firebase Hosting**, and **Firestore**. I verified checkout and other critical paths **end-to-end before handover**, including real payment flows. The **client did not take it live** afterward — which underscored that freelance outcomes depend on factors outside engineering, and that I care about shipping complete work even when adoption is not guaranteed.

The key insight from freelancing was that I prefer working in small teams where I can share clever solutions with people who understand them and share difficult challenges with teammates. The isolation of freelancing made me realize I need collaborative environments. I need people around me who appreciate good technical work, who can provide feedback and perspective, and who share the excitement of solving problems together.

This realization led me back to Nurtur (the same company as BriefYourMarket.com, but under a different contract structure) in July 2022. I wanted the collaborative environment, the technical challenges, and the opportunity to work with a team again. The freelance period had served its purpose - it taught me what I needed, and I was ready to return to team-based work.

The freelance experience wasn't a failure - it was valuable learning. I discovered my preferences, learned what energizes me versus what drains me, and gained clarity about the work environment I need to do my best work. These insights have informed my career decisions since and help me evaluate opportunities more effectively.

What I took from freelancing was the understanding that I'm at my best when I'm part of a team, working on technical challenges, collaborating with others who understand the work, and focusing on building excellent systems rather than managing client relationships. This clarity has been valuable in making career decisions and understanding what I need to thrive professionally.
