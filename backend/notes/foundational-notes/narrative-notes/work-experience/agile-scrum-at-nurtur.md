# Agile and Scrum at Nurtur

## Daily Practice

At Nurtur, we followed Agile methodologies with daily or twice-weekly standups depending on the team. The frequency varied by project phase and team needs, but the structure was consistent.

**Standups**: In some teams we had standups every morning, in others twice a week. The format was standard: everyone shared what they worked on the day before (or since the last standup) and what they were working on today. It typically took about 20 minutes.

**Purpose beyond status**: Standups weren't just status updates. They were an opportunity to ask for help when stuck, give leadership an idea of the project's direction, and surface problems before they became too big or embarrassing. Unspoken problems grew into blockers, so the standup ritual created regular opportunities to raise issues early.

## Tools and Tracking

When I started at Nurtur, we used TFS (Team Foundation Server) to track work. By the time I left in February 2026, the company had migrated almost entirely to Jira.

We used **sprints and work items**. Work was broken down into items that could be completed within a sprint. Items were assigned to developers and tracked on boards. This gave everyone visibility into what was in progress, what was blocked, and what was coming next.

## Example: Email Editor Project

The Email Editor project demonstrates how this worked in practice:

**Setup**: When the project started, a new Jira board was created specifically for the Email Editor. Work items were defined and assigned to each person on the 4-person team to fill a sprint.

**Daily rhythm**: We met for standups every morning to quickly discuss progress. These typically took 20 minutes. Then we spent the rest of the day working on our assigned items.

**Progression**: The project progressed in this rhythm - plan the sprint, break work into items, assign items to team members, daily standups to coordinate, work on items, complete the sprint, repeat. This steady cadence kept the team aligned and the project moving forward.

## What Worked

The Agile approach at Nurtur worked well because:

- **Regular communication**: Daily or twice-weekly standups prevented silos and kept everyone aware of progress and blockers.
- **Visibility**: Jira boards gave everyone visibility into the project state without needing to ask.
- **Accountability**: Work items and sprint commitments created natural accountability without being heavy-handed.
- **Early problem detection**: The standup ritual caught problems when they were still small and manageable.

## Adapting to Team Needs

The flexibility to have daily standups for some teams and twice-weekly for others showed the organization understood that Agile practices should serve the team, not the other way around. Teams in rapid development phases had more frequent standups. Teams in maintenance or slower phases met less often.

This pragmatic approach to Agile - following the principles but adapting the practices to fit the actual work - made it effective rather than bureaucratic.
