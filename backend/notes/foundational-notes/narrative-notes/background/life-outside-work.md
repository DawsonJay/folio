# Life Outside Work

## What I Do Now

I do silverworking, bookbinding, and baking. I also volunteer at a community garden. These are all hands-on, detail-oriented activities that require patience and craftsmanship - similar mindset to coding but in completely different domains.

I like hiking a lot - one of the reasons I moved to Canada was the access to wilderness and natural landscapes. I also run regularly.

A lot of my time recently has been spent making projects and setting up this portfolio, so some hobbies have fallen by the side temporarily, but they're activities I return to when I have space.

## What Still Matters from the Past

Three things from my past continue to shape how I work and who I am:

**Art**: At my core, I think of myself as creative - someone who makes things as their way of expressing themselves. The art background isn't just history; it's a fundamental part of my identity. I approach coding as a creative act, not just a technical one.

**Kayaking competitions**: They taught me how to approach challenges. Having a clear goal and working in sprints with a singular focus. The competitive kayaking experience gave me the ability to focus intensely on a specific objective and work systematically toward it. That discipline transfers directly to how I approach technical projects.

**Teaching**: This shapes how I approach people. Not in an educational way in the sense of instructing, but in wanting to support them and help them flourish, giving them the environment they need to be the best versions of themselves. This is why people called me "team dad" at university and why I gravitate toward mentoring at work.

## The Order of Things

I think about priorities in three tiers:

1. **Professional work comes first**: This is where I create value and build my career. It's the foundation.

2. **Everything I do outside of work to maintain health and sanity**: Relationships and exercise. These aren't optional or "if I have time" activities. They're essential to being someone who can do good work sustainably.

3. **Personal products**: The things I make for myself, like WhatNow or this portfolio. These are meaningful but come after the first two tiers are handled.

This hierarchy keeps me balanced. Professional work matters, but it can't come at the expense of health and relationships. Personal projects are valuable but need to fit around the essentials.

## Connection to Work

My life outside work isn't separate from my professional identity - it supports it. Hiking and connection to nature provide energy and perspective. Moving to Canada was partly about getting access to the kind of environment that keeps me grounded - the wilderness here is something I genuinely wanted in my life.

The creative identity from art, the focused approach from kayaking competitions, and the supportive mindset from teaching all show up in how I work. They're not just hobbies or history - they're fundamental to who I am as a developer and team member.
